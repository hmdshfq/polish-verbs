Chapter 09 · rekcja — which case a verb wants

Verbs and the cases
the case is part of the verb

Conjugating a verb correctly is only half of using it. The other half is the case of what follows it, and that case is a property of the verb itself — you learn it together with the word, the way you learn its gender in other languages. This chapter is the list, with the reasoning behind each group.

01

The principle

Polish calls this rekcja — government. A verb “governs” the case of its object. There is no way to guess it from English, and translating word by word will produce the wrong case more often than the right one. The safe habit is the one Polish children are taught: learn the verb with a short phrase, never alone.

Why this saves you time

There are only five patterns, and four of them are the plain cases you already know. The whole of this chapter is a list of which verbs go where. Once a verb is filed under its case, it stays there for good.

02

Accusative — the plain object

This is the default. If nothing in the meaning of the verb pushes towards another case, the object is accusative. Most verbs of having, seeing, reading, eating, buying and doing live here.

03

Why szukać takes the genitive

The genitive group is not random. These verbs all share an idea of not having the whole thing: wanting, lacking, seeking, needing, being afraid, listening to (taking in part of something), learning (taking in part of a body of knowledge). The genitive is the case of “some of it, not all of it”, and these verbs live inside that meaning.

VerbWhat followsWhy the genitive

potrzebowaćPotrzebuję pomocy.a need is a lack

zapominaćZapominam słów.losing hold of something
życzyćŻyczę ci szczęścia.wishing for something not yet had
The trap for English speakers

Szukam kluczy — not szukam klucze. Słucham muzyki — not słucham muzykę. “Search for” and “listen to” feel transitive in English, so the accusative feels right. It is not.

04

Dative — the person affected

The dative marks the person something is done for or to, especially where the action helps, harms, pleases or answers them.

VerbExampleMeaning
pomagaćPomagam mamie.I help my mother.
dziękowaćDziękuję panu.Thank you, sir.
wierzyćWierzę ci.I believe you.
odpowiadaćOdpowiadam nauczycielowi.I answer the teacher.
podobać sięPodoba mi się Łódź.I like Łódź. (Łódź pleases me.)
smakowaćSmakuje ci kawa?Do you like the coffee?
zazdrościćZazdroszczę ci.I envy you.
Podobać się turns the sentence around

English says “I like Łódź”; Polish says “Łódź is pleasing to me”. So the thing liked is the subject and the person is in the dative: Podoba mi się Łódź. The same shape appears in Smakuje mi kawa — the coffee tastes good to me. Getting used to this is one of the moments Polish starts to feel like a different language rather than a coded English.

05

Instrumental — being, becoming, using

The instrumental marks the means and the identity. After być and zostać it names what someone is; with verbs of managing and interest it names what you are occupied with.

VerbExampleMeaning
byćJestem programistą.I am a programmer.
zostaćZostałem nauczycielem.I became a teacher.
interesować sięInteresuję się historią.I am interested in history.
zajmować sięZajmuję się dziećmi.I look after the children.
kierowaćKieruję firmą.I run the company.
nazywać sięNazywam się Anna.My name is Anna.
Adjectives stay nominative

The noun after być is instrumental, but an adjective describing the subject still agrees in the nominative: Jestem zmęczony, not zmęczonym. Compare Jestem dobrym programistą — there the adjective modifies the instrumental noun, so it goes instrumental too.

06

Verb plus preposition

Most intransitive verbs take a fixed preposition, and the preposition decides the case. Learn the three words as one unit: czekać na autobus, not czekać.

The prepositions and their cases

na + accusative (waiting for, counting on), o + locative (thinking about, asking for), z + instrumental (agreeing with, laughing at), do + genitive (going to), od + genitive (depending on), za + instrumental in dziękować za. The case belongs to the preposition, not to the verb.

07

Negation: accusative becomes genitive

This is the one case rule that applies to every verb, not to a list. When a direct object would be accusative in an affirmative sentence, it becomes genitive once the verb is negated.

{{negation}}
The mistake this causes

Nie mam czasu, not nie mam czas. Nie znam tej osoby, not tę osobę. Nie widzę nikogo — and notice that nikt goes genitive too, and the double negative is correct Polish.

08

Quantity also takes the genitive

Even without nie, an object that is a quantity rather than a whole thing takes the genitive: dużo, mało, trochę, kilka, and numbers above four.

PolishEnglishWhy
Kupuję chleb.I am buying bread.accusative — the whole object
Kupuję dużo chleba.I am buying a lot of bread.genitive — a quantity
Mam pięć lat.I am five years old.genitive — a number above four
Wypiłem szklankę wody.I drank a glass of water.genitive — a portion of something
Numbers, in one line

One takes the nominative, two to four the nominative plural, and five and above the genitive plural: jeden kot, dwa koty, pięć kotów. It is the same “part of a whole” idea, applied to counting.

09

Five traps

1
Translating the case from English. Słucham muzykę feels right and is wrong: słucham muzyki.
2
Forgetting the genitive after nie. Nie mam czasu, nie znam tej osoby.
3
A nominative adjective after być plus the instrumental noun. Jestem dobrym programistą — the adjective agrees with the noun it describes, not with the subject.
4
Dropping the preposition. Czekam autobus is not Polish; it is czekam na autobus.
5
Expecting podobać się to work like “like”. The thing is the subject and the person is dative: Podoba mi się Łódź.
10

What to take away

  • Learn the verb with a short phrase, never alone: szukać kluczy, not szukać.
  • Accusative is the default object; genitive is for wanting, lacking, fearing and seeking.
  • Dative is the person affected; instrumental is being, becoming and using.
  • A negated object always goes genitive: nie mam czasu.
  • Quantities go genitive too: dużo chleba, pięć kotów.
