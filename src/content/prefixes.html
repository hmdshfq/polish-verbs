Chapter 11 · one root, a family of verbs

Prefixes and families
pisanie, spisanie, opisanie

Here is the fact that turns Polish vocabulary from a list into a system: most verbs are built from a small root plus a prefix. Learn the roots and what the prefixes do, and you can guess the meaning and the aspect of words you have never seen. Pisać is one root; napisać, przepisać, podpisać, wypisać, odpisać are all built on it.

01

Two things a prefix can do

Adding a prefix to an imperfective verb does one of two things, and telling them apart is the skill of this chapter.

What the prefix doesExampleResult
completes it — same meaning, now perfectivepisać → napisaća pair: “to write” / “to write and finish”
changes it — a new meaning altogetherpisać → podpisaća new verb: “to sign”
The test

Ask: does the prefixed verb still mean the same basic action? Napisać is still writing, so it is the perfective partner. Podpisać is signing, a different act, so it is a new word — and it has its own imperfective, podpisywać.

02

The prefixes and their senses

A prefix is not a random syllable: each one carries an old spatial meaning — in, out, through, up to, away — and that meaning survives, more or less clearly, in the modern verbs. The table gives the sense and the relatives built on the same prefix.

Read the “also” line

Each prefix has a family. Wy- means “out, to the end”, and it gives wypić (drink up), wyjść (go out), wypisać (write out). Once the sense is in your head, the family stops being a list to memorise and becomes a pattern to recognise.

03

Four families, side by side

The clearest way to see the system is to take one root and lay its prefixes out. Here is pisać and jechać, each with six relatives.

The prefix is the same; the verb is not

Compare napisać (“write and finish”, a partner of pisać) with najechać (“run into”, a new verb on a different root meaning). The prefix behaves the same way, but only one of the results is a partner. This is why a prefix cannot be applied mechanically.

04

Making the perfective, and then the imperfective again

A prefixed verb is normally perfective. If you then need the imperfective of that new verb — for a habit, or a process — you add a suffix instead. This gives a two-step family, and it is very common in real Polish.

StepVerbAspectMeaning
the rootpisaćimpfto write
+ prefixnapisaćpfto write (and finish)
+ prefix, new meaningpodpisaćpfto sign
+ suffix on the new verbpodpisywaćimpfto be signing, to sign repeatedly
the root, new meaningczytaćimpfto read
+ prefixprzeczytaćpfto read through

The direction to remember

Prefix makes a perfective. Suffix makes an imperfective. So a longer word is not always the perfective — kupować is longer than kupić and is the imperfective. The rule is about the direction of the step, not about the length.

05

When a prefix stops meaning anything

Some very common prefixed verbs have lost the spatial sense completely. Zrobić is not “do down”; it is simply the perfective of robić. Napisać is not “write onto”; it is the plain perfective. For these, the prefix is a grammatical marker, not a word.

PrefixVerbIts partnerIs the prefix meaningful?
z-zrobićrobićno — pure completion

Do not over-read a prefix

A learner who knows that prze- means “through” may expect przepraszać to mean something like “ask through”. It means “to apologise”. The prefix system is a guide to meaning, not a code you can decode word by word.

06

How to use this when you meet a new verb

1
Find the root. Strip the prefix and see whether a verb you know is underneath: przepisać → pisać.
2
Guess the sense from the prefix. prze- = through, so przepisać is likely “write through” — copy out.
3
Check the aspect. A prefixed verb is normally perfective. If the dictionary says impf, it has a suffix too.
4
Look for its own partner. If the meaning changed, the new verb needs its own imperfective — podpisać / podpisywać.
5
Confirm it in the verb finder. A guess is a guess until a table confirms it; this site's verb finder gives the verified forms and the source.
07

What to take away

  • A prefix either completes a verb (same meaning, now perfective) or makes a new verb (new meaning).
  • Prefix senses are spatial: w- in, wy- out, prze- through, od- away, do- up to, pod- under.
  • Prefix makes perfective; suffix makes imperfective: podpisać → podpisywać.
  • Some prefixes are pure grammar and mean nothing: zrobić, napisać.
  • Use the prefix as a guess, then confirm the form in a table.
