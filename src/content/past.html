Chapter 04 · the tense that reports who spoke

The past tense
-ł-, then who you are

The Polish past is the easiest tense in the language to build: one stem, a handful of endings. It is also the one that makes learners hesitate, because every singular form says whether the speaker is a man or a woman. Both things are true at once — the shape is simple, the meaning is personal.

01

The -ł- stem

Take the infinitive, drop -ć, and add -ł. That gives the masculine third person singular. Add -a and you have the feminine, add -o the neuter. Everything else in the tense is built on this same stem.

The infinitive stem survives

The -i- of robić and the -a- of pisać stay in the past: it is robił, never robł, and pisał, never pisł. The past is built on the infinitive, not on the present — which is why idę (I go) has the past szedłem (I went). Chapter 8 covers those irregular stems.

02

The four endings worth naming

-em = I (a man), -am = I (a woman), -eś = you (a man), -aś = you (a woman). The plural adding -śmy and -ście at the very end: pisa-li-śmy, pisa-ły-śmy. The ending is always last.

03

Gender is not optional

In English, “I wrote” says nothing about the speaker. In Polish, pisałem says a man said it and pisałam says a woman did. This is not a stylistic choice — a woman cannot say byłem, and a man cannot say byłam about himself. Polish speakers feel this as part of the word, the way English speakers feel “he” and “she” are different words.

SentenceMeaningWho is speaking
Wczoraj czytałem książkę.I read a book yesterday.a man
Wczoraj czytałam książkę.I read a book yesterday.a woman
Wczoraj czytaliśmy książkę.We read a book yesterday.a group with at least one man
Wczoraj czytałyśmy książkę.We read a book yesterday.a group of women only
The plural rule that surprises everyone

A group of ten women and one man uses byliśmy, not byłyśmy. The masculine-personal form covers every group that contains a man, however many women are present. Only a group made entirely of women, children or things takes -ły-. The same split runs through adjectives and pronouns, so it is worth learning once and properly.

04

The negative and the ending

The ending normally sits at the end of the verb: czytałem, czytaliśmy. In front of nie, careful Polish keeps them apart — nie czytałem — while fast speech often welds the two into one word: nieczytałem. Both are heard; the two-word version is what you should write.

WrittenHeardMeaning
Nie czytałem tej książki.nieczytałemI (a man) did not read that book.
Nie czytałam tej książki.nieczytałamI (a woman) did not read that book.
Nie czytaliśmy tego.nieczytaliśmyWe (with a man) did not read it.
Negation changes the object's case

Notice nie czytałem tej książki, not tę książkę. A negated verb takes the genitive where the affirmative takes the accusative. Chapter 9 takes this apart; for now, let the example stand as it is.

05

Both aspects live in the past

The past tense does not decide the aspect — a past verb can be either half of a pair, and the choice changes what you are saying. This is where the aspect work of chapter 2 pays off.

Imperfective — a process, a habit, unfinishedPerfective — one act, completed
Wczoraj pisałem list. — I was writing a letter.Wczoraj napisałem list. — I wrote the letter (it is done).
Czytałem tę książkę. — I was reading it.Przeczytałem tę książkę. — I read it through.
Kupowałem chleb. — I was buying bread.Kupiłem chleb. — I bought the bread.
06

Watch the ę and the o

In wziąć the feminine and plural forms take ę where the masculine has ą: wziął but wzięła, wzięli. In móc the o narrows to ó before a soft ending: mógł but mogła. Chapter 8 has the full tables.

07

Four traps

1
One gender form for both. A woman says czytałam, a man czytałem. Writing -em when you are a woman is the mistake Poles hear first, and it is worth practising aloud until it is automatic.
2
Treating “all women” as the default plural. One man anywhere in the group and it is byliśmy, czytaliśmy.
3
Putting the pronoun in front. Ja czytałem is emphatic. The ending already carries the person, so the plain sentence starts with the verb or the time: Wczoraj czytałem.
4
Assuming the past is always completed. “I was reading” and “I read it” are both past — czytałem and przeczytałem. Aspect, not tense, decides.
08

What to take away

  • Infinitive minus -ć, plus -ł / -ła / -ło, then the personal ending.
  • -em and -am for I; -eś and -aś for you; -śmy and -ście go last in the plural.
  • Any group containing a man takes the masculine-personal plural: byliśmy.
  • Negation takes the genitive: nie czytałem tej książki.
  • Six verbs have an irregular past stem: iść, jeść, mieć, wziąć, móc and their relatives.
