Chapter 08 · the verbs that carry the language

The irregular verbs
fifteen, and all of them common

Polish is remarkably regular, and even its irregular verbs are irregular in patterns rather than at random. The fifteen below are the ones you cannot avoid: być, mieć, iść, jeść, wziąć and their relatives. Every one has a full table on this site, and every form came from a dictionary inflection table.

01

What “irregular” means here

A Polish verb is called irregular when its stems differ from each other — the infinitive, the present and the past are built on different roots. That is all. The endings stay regular: once you have the stem, the six persons are the ones you already know.

02

być — to be

The most irregular verb in the language, and the one you cannot do without. Its present comes from jest-, its past from by-, and its future from będ-: three shapes for one verb.

{{conj:być}}
nie ma — “there is no”

For existence, the negative of jest is not nie jest but nie ma, followed by the genitive: Nie ma mleka — there is no milk. Nie ma czasu — there is no time. Jest is for a property or a location (Jest zimno, Jest w domu); nie ma is for the absence of a thing.

The case after być

A profession or a state with być takes the instrumental: Jestem programistą — I am a programmer. But an adjective stays in the nominative and agrees: Jestem zmęczony / zmęczona — I am tired. Chapter 9 has the full rule.

03

mieć — to have

A -m, -sz verb: there is no -ę anywhere, and the third person plural is mają, which follows none of the four conjugations. Learn the six as a chant.

{{conj:mieć}}
No perfective partner

Mieć is a state, so it has no perfective. Where English says “to get”, Polish uses a different verb: dostać. Dostałem list — I got a letter, and Mam list — I have a letter.

04

iść and jechać — to go

Polish splits “go” by how you travel. Iść is on foot; jechać is in or on a vehicle of any kind — car, bus, train, bicycle. Using the wrong one is not a small slip; it changes the picture completely.

The four-way split, in one sentence

Idę do pracy. — I am walking to work (right now, on foot). Chodzę do pracy. — I go to work on foot (every day). Jadę do pracy. — I am driving to work (now). Jeżdżę do pracy. — I drive to work (regularly).

05

jeść and pić — to eat and drink

Both are short and both change shape between the present and the past. Jeść has jem in the present and jadłem in the past; pić has piję and piłem.

{{conj:jeść}} {{conj:pić}}
Watch the third person plural

Jeść → jedzą, not “jeją”. Pić → piją. And in the past jedli (not “jedli” with a second e) but pili — two different patterns inside two very short words.

06

wziąć and brać — to take

A suppletive pair: the two halves share no root at all. Wziąć is perfective — so its present-looking forms are the future — and brać is the imperfective.

{{conj:wziąć}} {{conj:brać}}
The ą and ę swap

Wziął but wzięła, wzięło, wzięli, wzięły. The nasal vowel is ą only in the masculine singular and in the infinitive; everywhere else it is ę. The same swap appears in zacząć → zaczął / zaczęła and zamknąć → zamknął / zamknęła.

07

móc, musieć, chcieć

The three modal verbs. None has an ordinary perfective partner, and all three change their stem between the present and the past.

“Must” has no perfective

Polish cannot say “to have finished having to”. An obligation is always imperfective: muszę iść. For the completion of the action that follows, use the perfective of that verb: muszę to zrobić — I have to do it, and it will be done.

08

wiedzieć or znać?

English uses “know” for both, Polish never does. Wiedzieć is to know a fact — it is followed by a clause or by o. Znać is to know a person, a place, a language or a thing — it takes a direct object.

{{conj:wiedzieć}} {{conj:znać}}

The mistake to avoid

Znam, gdzie to jest is wrong — znać cannot take a clause. And wiem Łódź is wrong for the mirror reason. Learn the two with their shapes: wiem, że… / wiem, gdzie… and znam + object.

09

dać and dawać

A clean pair where the perfective is the shorter word. Dać is perfective, so dam is “I will give”. The past is regular on da-.

{{conj:dać}} {{conj:dawać}}
daj is the imperative

“Give me” is daj mi — and the same imperative serves both aspects in practice. Watch the third person plural: dadzą, not “dadą”.

Three “stand” verbs, three pictures

Stać — to be standing (a state). Stanąć — to take a stand, to come to a stop (one act, perfective). Stawiać — to put something upright, to stand something up. Choosing between them is an aspect decision, not a synonym choice.

12

Six traps

1
Nie jest for “there is no”. It is nie ma plus the genitive: nie ma mleka.
2
Jechać for walking. On foot is always iść, whatever the distance within the room.
3
Using the one-way verb for a habit. Every day on foot is chodzę, not idę.
4
Wiem Łódź. A place takes znam. A fact takes wiem.
5
Wziął / wzięła mixed up. The ą is only in the masculine singular and the infinitive.
6
A perfective after będę. Będę wziąć is not Polish. Wezmę is.
13

What to take away

  • “Irregular” means different stems, never different endings.
  • Być has three roots: jest- (present), by- (past), będ- (future).
  • Iść is on foot, jechać by vehicle; chodzić and jeździć are the repeated versions.
  • Wiedzieć is a fact, znać is a person, place or language.
  • Wziąć and brać share no root — learn them as two words.
