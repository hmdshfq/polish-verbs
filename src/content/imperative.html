Chapter 06 · commands, requests, and how Polish prefers to ask

The imperative
three forms, and a lot of politeness

The imperative is the smallest mood in Polish: three forms, no first or third person singular, and a stressing regularity that makes it easy to build. What is not small is its social side — Polish softens commands far more than English does, and a bare imperative can sound blunt where an English one sounds normal.

01

Three forms, three persons

The imperative exists only for ty, my and wy. There is no “I command” and no “he commands”; for those, Polish uses niech (section 5).

PersonpisaćrobićiśćWho it is for
typisz!rób!idź!one person you know
mypiszmy!róbmy!idźmy!let's…
wypiszcie!róbcie!idźcie!more than one person
The my and wy forms are built on the ty form

Once you have the ty form, the other two are mechanical: add -my for let's and -cie for a group. pisz → piszmy → piszcie, rób → róbmy → róbcie, idź → idźmy → idźcie. The same two endings you met in the present tense.

02

How the ty form is made

Take the third person plural of the present — the oni form — and strip its ending. What is left is the imperative stem.

oni — presentStrip the endingImperative ty
pisząpisz-pisz!
czytajączytaj-czytaj!
robiąrobi- → rób-rób!
mówiąmów-mów!
idąidź-idź!
kupująkupuj-kupuj!

Two things happen to the stem. If it ends in a hard consonant, the imperative stays as it is: pisz, mów, czytaj. If it ends in a soft consonant, no further ending is needed either — idź, siedź, chodź. And a cluster of consonants at the end can force a vowel in: robią → rób, biorą → bierz.

The -aj group is the easy one

Verbs in -ać that belong to conjugation III simply keep their -aj: czytać → czytaj, pytać → pytaj, mieszkać → mieszkaj, otwierać → otwieraj. If you are unsure of a form, and the verb is an -am / -asz verb, the safe guess is -aj.

03

The imperative of both aspects

Both halves of a pair have an imperative, and they mean different things. The imperfective command is about doing something, repeatedly or at all; the perfective command is about getting it done once.

robię → rób!zrobię → zrób!be doing it / get it done
kupuję → kupuj!kupię → kup!be buying / buy it
otwieram → otwieraj!otworzę → otwórz!open it (repeatedly) / open it now

05

niech — he, she, they

For the third person, and for polite address, Polish uses niech plus the present (or, for a perfective, the future) of the verb. This is also how you say “let him…”, “may she…” and how you give a respectful instruction to a stranger.

FormExampleMeaning
on / ona / onoNiech pisze.Let him write. / He should write.
oni / oneNiech piszą.Let them write.
pan / paniNiech pan siada. / Niech pani siada.Please, do sit down.
państwoNiech państwo siadają.Please, do sit down (to a group).
niech is the polite command

Niech pan siada is what a doctor says. Siadaj! is what you say to a dog, or to a friend in a hurry. The niech form is not old-fashioned or formal — it is the ordinary way to tell a stranger to do something without sounding rude.

06

Negative commands take the imperfective

This is the single most important rule on the page. A prohibition uses the imperfective, even when the positive command would use the perfective.

Do it — perfectiveDon't — imperfectiveMeaning
Zrób to!Nie rób tego!Do it! / Don't do it!
Napisz to!Nie pisz tego!Write it! / Don't write it!
Otwórz okno!Nie otwieraj okna!Open the window! / Don't open it!
Zadzwoń do niej!Nie dzwoń do niej!Call her! / Don't call her!
Why, and the exception

A prohibition is about a whole action, not about a result — so the aspect is imperfective. The exception is a warning about an accident, where the perfective is used to say “don't (accidentally) do it”: Nie zapomnij! (don't forget), Nie zgub kluczy! (don't lose the keys). Also note that the object switches to the genitive in a negative command: nie rób tego, nie otwieraj okna.

07

How Polish asks instead of ordering

A bare imperative in Polish is stronger than in English. In a shop, an office or a stranger's house you will almost always hear one of the softer shapes below.

How it is saidLiterallyWhen
Proszę poczekać.I ask (you) to wait.the standard polite instruction
Czy mógłby pan poczekać?Could you wait?very polite, conditional — chapter 7
Niech pan poczeka.Let sir wait.polite, a little formal
Proszę bardzo.Please, very much.“here you are”, “go ahead”
Przepraszam, czy można…?Excuse me, is it possible to…?asking permission
The magic word is proszę

Proszę does an enormous amount of work in Polish: it means “please”, “here you are”, “go ahead”, and — followed by an infinitive — “please do this”. When you are not sure how to phrase a request politely, proszę plus the infinitive will almost always be right: Proszę zamknąć drzwi — please close the door.

Some verbs barely use it

A few imperative forms are grammatically real but practically never said. Móż (from móc) is one: mathematicians “let x be…” use it, nobody orders anyone to be able. Bądź is different — it is alive and common in farewells and fixed phrases: Bądź zdrów (take care), bądź uprzejmy (be so kind).

09

Five traps

1
Using a perfective after nie. Nie rób, never nie zrób. The accident warnings (nie zapomnij) are the only common exception.
2
A bare imperative to a stranger. Proszę poczekać or Niech pan poczeka, not Poczekaj! to a shop assistant.
3
Putting się in the wrong place. Ucz się!, not Ucz się! with się between verb and ending — and definitely not się ucz in a single-word command.
4
Forgetting the genitive after a negative command. Nie otwieraj okna, not okno; nie pisz tego, not to.
5
Making the imperative from the infinitive. It comes from the oni form of the present: robić → robią → rób, not robi.
10

What to take away

  • Three forms: ty, my, wy. Add -my and -cie to the ty form.
  • The ty form comes from the oni present, minus its ending.
  • Niech gives the third person and the polite command: Niech pan siada.
  • Negative commands take the imperfective and the genitive: nie rób tego.
  • Bare imperatives are blunt. Proszę + infinitive is the everyday polite form.
